How Much Does a Trip to Europe Cost?

VelvetShadow
01.07.2026 • Read time: 4 min

Planning a trip across the Atlantic is an exciting milestone, but the first and most common question every traveler asks is: How much does a trip to Europe cost? The truth is, there is no single magic number. A European vacation can cost anywhere from $50 a day for a rugged backpacker sleeping in dorms to over $500 a day for a luxury traveler seeking five-star hotels and Michelin-starred dining. Your final budget will depend heavily on your travel style, the duration of your stay, the specific countries you visit, and how well you manage your expenses.

To help you plan your finances realistically, this comprehensive guide breaks down the true cost of traveling to Europe by category—including flights, accommodation, food, transportation, and activities—while providing actionable tips to keep your wallet happy.

Average Cost Estimates by Travel Style

Before diving into individual expense categories, it helps to look at daily budget averages. These figures exclude international flights but cover lodging, food, local transport, and sightseeing.

Travel Style Daily Budget (USD) What It Includes
Budget / Backpacker $50 – $90 Hostel dorms, grocery store meals, street food, public transit, free walking tours.
Mid-Range / Comfortable $150 – $300 Private Airbnb or 3-star hotel rooms, casual restaurants, mix of public transit and occasional taxis, paid museum entries.
Luxury / High-End $500+ 4-to-5-star hotels, fine dining, private tours, rental cars or first-class train tickets.

1. International Flights and Getting There

For most travelers outside of Europe, long-haul flights represent one of the single largest upfront expenses. Round-trip airfare from North America to major European hubs like London, Paris, or Frankfurt typically ranges from $600 to $1,400, depending heavily on the season.

  • Peak Season (June – August): Expect the highest prices, often exceeding $1,200 for economy tickets.
  • Shoulder Season (April – May, September – October): Weather is still pleasant, but flight prices drop significantly, often falling into the $700 – $900 range.
  • Low Season (November – March): Aside from the Christmas holiday rush, this is when you will find the cheapest flights, sometimes as low as $500 round-trip.

Pro tip: Use flexible date search tools like Google Flights or Skyscanner, and book your transatlantic flights 3 to 6 months in advance to secure the best rates.

2. Accommodation Costs

Where you sleep dictates a massive portion of your travel expenditure. European cities offer an immense variety of lodging choices, but prices vary wildly by region. Eastern Europe (like Poland, Hungary, and Romania) offers incredible value, whereas Western and Northern Europe (like Switzerland, Norway, the UK, and Iceland) will stretch your budget to the limit.

  • Hostels: $30 – $60 per night for a dorm bed. Excellent for solo travelers looking to meet people.
  • Budget Hotels & Airbnbs: $80 – $150 per night for a private room or entire modest apartment.
  • Mid-Range Hotels: $150 – $300 per night for standard 3-to-4-star accommodations with modern amenities.
  • Luxury Hotels: $400+ per night for high-end boutique properties or globally recognized luxury brands.

3. Food and Drink Expenses

The culinary scene is one of the greatest joys of exploring Europe, and how you choose to eat will heavily influence your overall expenditure. You can easily spend €100 a day on upscale dining, or keep costs down to €20 a day by adopting local habits.

If you are mapping out your daily schedule, it is vital to learn how to avoid tourist traps in major European cities, especially regarding restaurants situated directly next to famous landmarks, which often charge double for subpar food.

  • Breakfast: Many budget hotels and hostels include breakfast. If not, a pastry and coffee at a local café will cost around €3 – €6.
  • Lunch: Grab-and-go options like boulangerie sandwiches, local street food, or a daily set-lunch menu (menu del día) cost roughly €8 – €15.
  • Dinner: Sitting down at a casual mid-range restaurant for a proper meal with a glass of house wine or beer typically runs €25 – €50 per person.

4. Internal Transportation: Trains, Buses, and Flights

Getting around Europe once you arrive is wonderfully efficient, thanks to high-speed rail networks, budget airlines, and long-distance coach services.

If you are planning your itinerary efficiently, you can check out the ultimate 2-week Europe itinerary for beginners to map out logical routes that minimize backtracking and expensive transit fees. Train travel is scenic and comfortable, but booking in advance is crucial. A last-minute high-speed train ticket between Paris and London or Rome and Florence can cost upwards of €150, whereas booking months ahead might net you a ticket for €40.

Budget airlines like Ryanair and EasyJet offer incredibly cheap fares—sometimes as low as €20—but always factor in hidden baggage fees and the cost of traveling to distant secondary airports.

5. Sightseeing, Activities, and Entertainment

Entrance fees to world-class museums, historical monuments, and guided tours add up quickly. While strolling through European plazas, parks, and architecture is completely free, ticking off bucket-list attractions requires financial planning.

  • Free Activities: Walking around historic districts, hiking public trails, visiting many public art galleries and cathedrals (e.g., Notre-Dame, Pantheon).
  • Standard Attractions: Major sites like the Colosseum in Rome, the Louvre in Paris, or the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam generally cost €15 to €30 per ticket.
  • Tours and Excursions: Day trips, wine-tasting experiences, or specialized guided tours typically range from €50 to €150 per person.

Practical Strategies to Cut Costs

Even if you choose to visit expensive destinations, smart planning can drastically lower your daily expenditure without sacrificing the quality of your experience. Implementing clever financial strategies is key to keeping your bank account intact. For a deeper dive into saving money, review comprehensive tactics on how to travel Europe on a budget to master the art of affordable globetrotting.

Summary Checklist for Your Budget:

  • Book flights 3 to 6 months in advance.
  • Travel during the shoulder season (spring or autumn).
  • Mix paid attractions with free outdoor sightseeing.
  • Eat your main meal at lunch when restaurants offer discounted fixed-price menus.
  • Use public transportation instead of taxis or rideshares.

Ultimately, a two-week trip to Europe can comfortably cost anywhere from $1,500 to $4,500+ per person, excluding international airfare, depending entirely on the choices you make. By setting a realistic budget and tailoring your itinerary to match your financial goals, you can experience the magic of Europe without the financial hangover.
