What Is the Cheapest European Country to Visit?

EchoChamber
01.06.2026 • Read time: 5 min

Between soaring airfares, historic hotels, and the temptation of fine dining, a trip across the Atlantic can quickly drain your savings. Fortunately, you can easily compare affordable flight and hotel deals right here. However, the Old Continent is a massive and economically diverse region. While iconic capitals like London, Paris, and Zurich can easily break the bank, Eastern and Southern Europe harbor incredible destinations where your money stretches remarkably far.

Whether you are planning the ultimate 2-week Europe itinerary for beginners or searching for off-the-beaten-path locations, knowing where to go is the key to affordable travel. In this comprehensive guide, we will break down the absolute cheapest European countries to visit, analyzing daily budgets, food costs, accommodation, and hidden gems.

Understanding the Cost of Travel in Europe

Before diving into specific countries, it is important to understand how travel expenses work in Europe. The continent is divided into two primary economic zones: the Eurozone (countries using the Euro) and non-Eurozone countries. Generally speaking, nations that retain their own currencies—such as Poland, Hungary, and Romania—tend to offer a lower cost of living and, by extension, much cheaper travel experiences for foreign tourists.

When calculating the cost of a destination, budget travelers typically look at three main pillars:

  • Accommodation: Hostels, budget guesthouses, and Airbnb rates.
  • Food & Drink: Street food, local grocery stores, casual cafes, and mid-range restaurants.
  • Transportation: Public transit, regional trains, buses, and inter-city travel.

Top Contenders: The Cheapest European Countries

Based on comprehensive cost indexes, backpacking data, and tourism expenditure reports, several nations consistently rank as the most budget-friendly in Europe.

1. North Macedonia

Often overlooked by mainstream tourists, North Macedonia is arguably the absolute cheapest country to visit in Europe. Located in the heart of the Balkans, it offers stunning natural beauty, rich history, and incredible hospitality at a fraction of Western European prices.

In the capital city of Skopje or by the stunning shores of Lake Ohrid, you can easily get by on a daily budget of $30 to $40 USD. A hearty meal at a traditional restaurant can cost as little as $5 to $8, and a pint of local beer is often under $2.

2. Bulgaria

Bulgaria is a perennial favorite among budget travelers. Boasting a diverse landscape that includes the dramatic peaks of the Rila Mountains, vibrant Black Sea resort towns like Varna and Burgas, and a history-rich capital in Sofia, Bulgaria delivers massive value.

Public transportation is exceptionally cheap, and hostel beds or budget private rooms can be found for meager rates. If you are compiling a list of the best places to visit in Europe for first-timers who are mindful of their wallets, Bulgaria’s capital Sofia deserves a top spot for its walkability and affordability.

3. Romania

From the medieval charm of Brașov and Sibiu to the legendary, misty castles of Transylvania, Romania feels like stepping into a fairy tale. Fortunately, it is also one of the most budget-friendly destinations on the continent.

While larger cities like Bucharest are slightly more expensive, they are still remarkably cheap compared to Western Europe. Visitors can enjoy hearty soups, grilled meats, and local pastries for just a few dollars. Train travel across the country is inexpensive, making it ideal for slow travelers looking to explore historical architecture without a massive budget.

4. Albania

Often dubbed the "Maldives of Europe" due to the breathtaking, crystal-clear turquoise waters of the Albanian Riviera, Albania has rapidly emerged as a must-visit Mediterranean destination. Despite its rising popularity, it remains remarkably inexpensive.

Coastal towns like Sarandë and Ksamil offer stunning beaches, fresh seafood, and affordable seaside apartments. Inland, cities like Gjirokastër and Berat showcase stunning Ottoman-era architecture and deep cultural heritage. If you venture slightly off the main tourist trail, you will find some of the top 10 hidden gems in Europe for an unforgettable summer vacation nestled within Albania's rugged northern mountains, such as the village of Theth.

5. Poland

As one of the larger economies in Central Europe, Poland might seem like an unexpected entry, but it remains vastly more affordable than Germany, France, or Scandinavia. Cities like Kraków, Wrocław, and Gdańsk offer world-class museums, magnificent market squares, and a thriving culinary scene at budget prices.

Poland is famous for its "Milk Bars" (*Bar Mleczny*)—state-subsidized cafeterias left over from the communist era that serve massive, delicious portions of traditional Polish comfort food (like pierogi and borscht) for just a couple of dollars.

Tips for Traveling Europe on a Budget

Choosing a cheap country is only half the battle. To truly maximize your savings while traveling through Eastern or Southern Europe, keep these practical tips in mind:

  • Travel in the Shoulder Season: Avoid July and August when European schools are out and prices peak. Instead, visit in May, June, September, or October for great weather and lower accommodation rates.
  • Eat Where the Locals Eat: Avoid restaurants located directly in front of major tourist landmarks. Walk a few blocks away to find neighborhood taverns, bakeries, and street food vendors.
  • Utilize Buses and Regional Trains: High-speed international rail passes can be expensive. In cheap European countries, long-distance buses (like FlixBus or local operators) and regional trains offer unbeatable rates.
  • Stay in Locally Owned Guesthouses: While chain hotels exist, staying in family-run guesthouses (*sobas* in the Balkans or *pensiuni* in Romania) often includes warm hospitality, local tips, and lower prices than standard hotels.

Ultimately, experiencing the rich culture, stunning landscapes, and deep history of Europe does not require a trust fund. By steering away from the typical tourist hubs and setting your sights on incredible, affordable destinations like North Macedonia, Bulgaria, Romania, or Albania, you can enjoy an unforgettable European adventure without the financial stress.
