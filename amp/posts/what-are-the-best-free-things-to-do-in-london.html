What are the best free things to do in London?

AetherDrifter
04.08.2026

London is universally famous for being a dynamic, fast-paced, and utterly fascinating metropolis, but it also carries a notorious reputation for being overwhelmingly expensive. From West End theatre tickets to pints at historic pubs and luxury shopping in Mayfair, your travel funds can deplete in the blink of an eye. However, beneath the veneer of high costs lies a well-kept secret: London offers some of the most phenomenal cultural, historical, and visual experiences in the entire world completely free of charge. If you are planning a grand continental adventure—perhaps figuring out How to Do Europe in 10 Days: The Ultimate Itinerary—carving out a few days specifically for the British capital without breaking the bank is an absolute must.

Whether you are a solo backpacker watching every penny, a family seeking affordable school-holiday entertainment, or a savvy traveler looking to balance out pricey destinations, London’s bounty of gratis attractions will astound you. Let’s dive deep into the ultimate guide detailing the absolute best free things to do in London.

World-Class Museums and Galleries (Zero Entry Fee)

One of London’s greatest cultural triumphs is that its premier national museums and art galleries are permanently free to enter for general exhibitions. While special temporary exhibitions may require a ticket, the permanent collections alone can easily consume entire days of your itinerary.

The British Museum

Located in Bloomsbury, the British Museum houses a vast collection of world art and artifacts spanning two million years of human history. You can marvel at the Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies, and the Parthenon Sculptures without spending a single penny. Be sure to look up and admire the breathtaking glass and steel roof of the Great Court.

Natural History Museum and Science Museum

Situated right next to each other in South Kensington, these two institutions are architectural masterpieces. The Natural History Museum greets you with Hope the blue whale suspended from the ceiling and offers incredible dinosaur galleries. Just steps away, the Science Museum lets you touch real-space history, explore the history of communication, and marvel at vintage steam engines.

Tate Modern and Tate Britain

For art lovers, London is a playground. Tate Modern, housed in a former power station on the South Bank, showcases contemporary masterpieces by artists like Picasso, Warhol, and Hockney. When you want a shift in tone, take the Tate boat down the Thames to Tate Britain, which houses the world's largest collection of historic British art, including iconic works by J.M.W. Turner.

Embrace the Great Outdoors: London's Royal Parks

Despite being a dense concrete jungle, London is remarkably green. The city's Royal Parks provide sprawling, manicured sanctuaries where you can escape the urban rush, feed parakeets, and soak up local life.

Hyde Park and Kensington Gardens

Stretching across the heart of the city, Hyde Park is massive. Take a stroll around the Serpentine lake, pay your respects at the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fountain, and walk over into Kensington Gardens to admire the ornate Albert Memorial.

Regent’s Park and Primrose Hill

Regent’s Park offers stunning rose gardens, boating lakes, and open-air theater spaces. If you walk north to the top of nearby Primrose Hill, you will be rewarded with one of the most romantic, unobstructed panoramic views of the London skyline.

Greenwich Park

Head slightly east to Greenwich for a double whammy of history and natural beauty. Climb up the hill to the Royal Observatory where you can stand on the Prime Meridian line (viewable from outside the paid enclosure) and gaze down at the majestic Old Royal Naval College and the River Thames.

Iconic London Views and Scenic Walks

You don't need to buy a ticket for the London Eye to get breathtaking views of the city. Some of the most iconic vistas in the world are completely accessible on foot.

  • The South Bank Walk: Stroll along the pedestrianized Thames path stretching from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge. You will pass street performers, skate parks, historic ships, and incredible angles of St. Paul’s Cathedral.
  • Sky Garden: While observation decks like the Shard cost a fortune, the top floors of 20 Fenchurch Street (known as the Walkie Talkie) feature a lush indoor garden with 360-degree views. Entry is completely free, though you must book a timed ticket online weeks in advance.
  • The Millennium Bridge: A stunning steel suspension footbridge that offers a direct, postcard-worthy photographic frame between Tate Modern on one side and St. Paul's Cathedral on the other.

Immersive Neighborhoods and Markets

Much of London's charm lives on its streets. Exploring the city's diverse, historic neighborhoods is entirely free and offers endless visual stimulation.

Covent Garden

Watch world-class street performers entertain crowds under the glass-roofed piazza, browse artisan crafts in the Apple Market, and soak in the vibrant atmosphere of central London's theater district.

Borough Market

While buying artisan cheeses, fresh pastries, and gourmet street food will cost you, simply wandering through London's oldest and most renowned food market is a sensory feast. The sights, smells, and historic railway architecture overhead make it a must-visit.

Notting Hill and Portobello Road

Famous for pastel-colored Victorian townhouses, quirky vintage shops, and the bustling Saturday Portobello Road Market, this neighborhood is ideal for a leisurely afternoon walk and countless Instagram photos.

Ceremonies, Culture, and History

London drips with royal heritage and historical pageantry that you can witness for free if you know where and when to look.

Changing of the Guard at Buckingham Palace

Watch the King’s Guard hand over duties at Buckingham Palace. Complete with red tunics and bearskin hats, this traditional military ceremony usually takes place on select mornings throughout the week (check the official British Army schedule online before you go).

Platform 9 ¾ at King’s Cross Station

Harry Potter fans can visit King’s Cross Station to see the famous luggage trolley disappearing into the wall between platforms 9 and 10. While taking a professional photo with the scarf costs money, queuing up to take your own photo with your smartphone is entirely free.

As you can see, experiencing the very best of the UK capital does not require a luxury budget. By mixing world-class free museums, royal green spaces, and historic walking routes, you can craft an unforgettable London getaway that leaves your wallet entirely intact.
