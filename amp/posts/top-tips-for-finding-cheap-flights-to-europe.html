Top Tips for Finding Cheap Flights to Europe

RetroPixel
02.07.2026

Dreaming of sipping espresso in Paris, admiring ancient architecture in Rome, or wandering through the cobblestone streets of historic capitals? For many travelers, the biggest hurdle to crossing the Atlantic is the high cost of airfare. However, with a strategic approach, modern technology, and a little bit of flexibility, flying to Europe on a budget is entirely achievable. Whether you are planning a fast-paced vacation utilizing how to do Europe in 10 days: the ultimate itinerary or setting out to explore the most walkable cities in Europe you must explore on foot, securing an affordable flight sets the foundation for a successful trip. This comprehensive guide breaks down the top tips and tricks for finding cheap flights to Europe.

1. Master the Art of Flexibility

The single most effective way to lower the price of your plane ticket is to remain flexible with your dates and destinations. Airlines adjust prices based on supply and demand, meaning certain days of the week and months of the year are significantly cheaper than others.

Fly During the Shoulder and Off-Peak Seasons

Summer is peak travel season in Europe. Millions of tourists flock to the continent, causing airfares and accommodation prices to skyrocket. To save hundreds of dollars, consider traveling during the shoulder season (spring and autumn) or the off-peak winter months.

  • Shoulder Season (April–May & September–October): Enjoy pleasant weather, smaller crowds, and much lower flight prices.
  • Off-Peak Season (November–March, excluding holidays): This is when you will find the absolute cheapest transatlantic fares, perfect for budget-conscious travelers.

Be Flexible with Days of the Week

Flying on weekends (Fridays and Sundays) is notoriously expensive because business travelers and weekend vacationers drive up demand. Instead, aim to fly on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, which are historically the cheapest days of the week to book air travel.

2. Utilize Advanced Flight Search Tools

Gone are the days of visiting individual airline websites one by one. Today, powerful flight aggregators do the heavy lifting for you, scanning hundreds of carriers in seconds.

3. Implement the "Open-Jaw" and "Two-Ticket" Strategies

If you are planning an extensive trip, standard round-trip tickets might not be your best financial option. Instead, look into creative ticketing methods.

Book Multi-City or Open-Jaw Tickets

An open-jaw ticket allows you to fly into one European city (e.g., London) and fly out of another (e.g., Amsterdam) without paying for two separate one-way tickets. This saves you both time and backtracking expenses, fitting seamlessly into itineraries like the safest European countries for solo travelers who want to traverse multiple borders safely.

The Hack-Flight (Self-Transfer) Method

Sometimes, booking a long-haul flight to a major hub like London, Frankfurt, or Paris, and then booking a separate low-cost European carrier (like Ryanair or EasyJet) to your final destination is much cheaper than booking the whole journey on a single legacy airline. Just be sure to leave a generous buffer time (at least 3 to 4 hours) between flights in case of delays.

4. Set Up Price Alerts and Track Trends

Flight prices fluctuate constantly—sometimes multiple times a day. You do not need to manually check prices every morning. Instead, let technology work for you.

Set up price alerts on Google Flights, Skyscanner, or Kayak for your target route. You will receive an email notification the moment prices drop or spike. Furthermore, monitor trends for at least two to three weeks before booking so you can recognize a genuine bargain when you see one.

5. Consider Alternate Airports

Major international hubs like London Heathrow (LHR), Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG), or Frankfurt (FRA) often come with high airport taxes and fees. Look into flying into secondary airports or nearby cities.

For example, if you want to visit London, check flights into Dublin, Amsterdam, or Brussels. From there, you can often catch a budget airline or a scenic train ride to your final destination for a fraction of the cost.

6. Leverage Airline Miles and Credit Card Points

If you travel with any frequency, signing up for a travel rewards credit card can drastically slash your travel costs. Many travel credit cards offer massive sign-up bonuses that can easily cover a round-trip ticket to Europe.

Additionally, always sign up for frequent flyer programs—even with airlines you rarely use. You can pool miles across airline alliances (like Star Alliance, Oneworld, or SkyTeam) to redeem free flights or cabin upgrades in the future.

Conclusion

Finding cheap flights to Europe is part science, part art, and a whole lot of patience. By remaining flexible with your travel dates, utilizing powerful aggregators, taking advantage of multi-city itineraries, and keeping an eye on alternative airports, you can save hundreds of dollars on airfare. Put these savings toward your accommodations, local cuisine, and unforgettable experiences, and you will be well on your way to the European adventure of a lifetime.
